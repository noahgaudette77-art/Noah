# INR Arcade, the finished game: what the owner asked for

A study game dressed as a 1980s/90s synthwave arcade. Three action games: Risk Blaster (a space rail shooter: the
player's starfighter fires at drones that carry the answers while a demon overlord attacks), Nest Egg Run (a road race:
the player's armoured war rig drives through the gate of the right answer) and Boss Rush (a duel with twelve bosses,
one per chapter, and a final one). Around them: a title screen, an arcade hall ("hub") with a cabinet for each game,
menus (chapter select, results, stats) and three quieter rooms (Memory Bank, Final Exam, Strategy Guide). A question
and its four answers are on screen during play.

The owner's words: "Shooting and being attacked must feel powerful, readable and varied." "Get the best-looking,
best-sounding version of this game possible." "Retro 1980s/90s synthwave arcade: neon magenta, cyan and violet,
glossy wet reflections, chrome, bloom, scanlines. Everything must feel like one coherent world. Originality is
mandatory: no copyrighted characters, logos or lookalikes." "All effects must keep text and answer buttons legible."

Mark each item `met`, `weak` or `missing`, with the file that shows it.

## Player weapons (Risk Blaster)
1. Bolts with a hot white core, a coloured glow and a tapered trail; a muzzle flash; a light that briefly lights the
   ship and what is near it.
2. A right answer: a bright impact burst with sparks, a shockwave ring, a bloom pulse and a small camera shake; the
   beam visibly connects ship and target.
3. A wrong answer: a distinct, darker fizzle (red-tinted sparks), so right and wrong are told apart without reading.
4. A rapid-fire feel: recoil, spent cells or sparks, heat on the barrels.

## Enemy and boss attacks (Risk Blaster, Boss Rush)
5. A clear telegraph before every attack: a wind-up glow, a warning marker, a short slow-motion beat.
6. Attack kinds (beam sweep, volley, slam, lock-on seekers, energy wave), each with its own colour, trail and impact;
   projectiles readable against the background and never over the question or the answers.
7. Taking a hit: a screen flash and colour split, a shake scaled to the damage, sparks and smoke, cracks or flicker
   on the HUD, a short blink, a heart lost with its own animation.
8. Low health: a pulsing red vignette, smoke or fire trailing from the ship or the vehicle.

## Boss reactions and endings
9. Hit flashes, staggers, parts breaking off, sparks and leaks, damage that grows across the fight.
10. A cinematic death: slow motion, chain explosions along the body, a final blast with a shockwave, debris flying,
    a reward burst of score, coins and stars.
11. Twelve distinct bosses, each original (none resembling a known character, robot or creature), each with its own
    look, attack flavour and colour.

## Nest Egg Run
12. Crashes and wrong-lane impacts with sparks, glass and debris, skid marks and a camera jolt; near misses with a
    whoosh and speed lines; boost flames and heat shimmer.
13. A war rig with one straight centreline, a symmetrical body, four wheels at the corners and no real car's grille
    or lights; traffic that resembles no real make.

## Every screen
14. Explosions built of layered fire, smoke, embers and light that falls on the ground and the models.
15. A sky for every stage and screen, lighting and reflected in the models; no seams or stretching.
16. The hub, the title, the menus and the three rooms with real 3D models and props (cabinets, trophies and medals,
    a vault and a deck, an exam podium, a great book), the same palette and light as the games.
17. One coherent world from screen to screen: palette, chrome and neon, light, HUD plates and type.
18. Every text and answer button readable at every moment, mid-effect included; nothing flashes more than three
    times a second.
